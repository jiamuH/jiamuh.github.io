Jiamu Huang

PhD student, Department of Physics, UC Santa Barbara

I’m a fifth-year PhD student in the Department of Physics at the University of California, Santa Barbara (UCSB), where I am a member of the ENIGMA Research Group, led by Professor Joseph Hennawi.

I am interested in understanding how galaxies and supermassive black holes formed and evolved in the early Universe, particularly during the first billion years after the Big Bang.

My research focuses on using observations from the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) to study high-redshift quasars, galaxies, and the large-scale structure of the early Universe. Through the ASPIRE and COSMOS-3D surveys, I investigate the spatial clustering of quasars and [O III]-emitting galaxies at redshifts z ~ 5–9. These measurements allow us to constrain the masses of their host dark matter halos and the fraction of time that supermassive black holes spend in their luminous quasar phase, providing insight into the connection between black hole growth, galaxy formation, and the underlying dark matter distribution.

I also study the physical conditions and structure of gas surrounding active galactic nuclei (AGN) through spectroscopic analysis, photoionization modeling, and continuum and emission-line reverberation mapping. In particular, I investigate how gas density, temperature, ionization state, and radiative transfer shape the observed emission-line spectra, including the transition between non-local thermodynamic equilibrium (non-LTE) and LTE regimes. By combining theoretical models with spectroscopic and time-domain observations, I aim to understand the physical properties and geometry of the broad-line region (BLR), as well as the structure of accretion disks around supermassive black holes.

Before UCSB, I was an undergraduate at UC Santa Cruz, where I studied AGN and star formation in their accretion disks through the chemical evolution of the AGN broad-line region, supervised by Professor Douglas Lin and Gregory Shields. I also work on AGN continuum and emission-line reverberation mapping with Professor Keith Horne at the University of St Andrews.

Outside of research, I enjoy astrophotography and photography more broadly, particularly capturing the night sky and the natural world. You can explore some of my work in my photography gallery. I am also a fan of skiing; some of my favorite spots are Mammoth Lakes in California, Alta in Utah, and Nagano in Japan.
